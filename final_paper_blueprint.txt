2. Polished Abstract (Honors Original Proposal + Integrates Follow-Up)

Medical Abbreviation Disambiguation (AD) is critical for clinical text parsing, yet existing methods either rely on rigid closed-vocabulary classifiers or require massive cloud LLMs that pose severe HIPAA and privacy risks. In this work, we evaluate a low-resource framework that reframes medical AD into a private, open-vocabulary text generation task on an edge-scale model (Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct) using Quantized Low-Rank Adaptation (QLoRA) and less than 0.1% (1,000 samples) of traditional training data. Across highly ambiguous multi-sense acronyms from the MeDAL benchmark, QLoRA fine-tuning achieves 76.00% Exact Match (EM) on seen abbreviations—surpassing fine-tuned BioClinicalBERT (37.00%) and PubMedBERT (54.40%)—while improving unseen acronym EM tenfold over zero-shot prompting (1.20% to 12.00%). To further map out practical deployment limits for GPU-less rural clinics, we conduct a post-hoc engineering comparison against a lightweight, closed-vocabulary TF-IDF context ranker (15,000 features) across 198,075 ambiguous instances. Operating with 0 MB GPU VRAM and 0.11 ms latency, the TF-IDF ranker reaches 79.71% Top-1 EM and 95.74% Top-3 accuracy, illustrating the trade-off between open-vocabulary neural generalization and ultra-lightweight lexical retrieval in clinical NLP.

4. Transition & Discussion Text (To Tie Both Experiments Together)

From Parametric Limits to Practical Clinic Deployment: Table 1 demonstrates that QLoRA fine-tuning enables an edge-scale 1B LLM to outperform dedicated biomedical encoders on seen acronyms (76.00% vs. 54.40% EM) while retaining non-zero generalization on unseen acronyms (12.00% EM). However, our hardware profiling highlights two practical barriers for small rural clinics: Llama-3.2-1B requires 5.1–5.9 GB of GPU VRAM and 75–284 ms per abbreviation, and an 88% error rate on unseen terms poses clinical safety risks if hallucinations are inserted unverified. Because real-world clinical shorthand is largely enumerable via standardized lexicons (e.g., UMLS), our post-hoc study in Table 2 shows that pairing a fixed dictionary with a 15,000-feature TF-IDF context ranker eliminates GPU requirements entirely (0 MB VRAM, 0.11 ms latency) while achieving 79.71% Top-1 EM and surfacing the correct expansion within the top three candidates 95.74% of the time.
